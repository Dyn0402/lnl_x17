## 2. The claims

1. **"excess near 140° in the decay of the 18.15 MeV 1⁺ state of ⁸Be, populated in ⁷Li(p,γ) at Ep = 1.03 MeV" [1].**
   Supported. The paper populates the states at "the Ep = 0.441 and 1.03 MeV resonances" and reports the deviation "at
   Θ ≈ 140°".
2. **"mass of 16.7 MeV" and "R = Γ_X/Γ_γ ≈ 5.8 × 10⁻⁶" [1].** Supported: 16.70 ± 0.35 (stat) ± 0.5 (syst) MeV/c², and a
   "boson to γ branching ratio of 5.8 × 10⁻⁶ ... for the best fit". The abstract wording was "They interpret the excess
   as a new boson". The 2016 paper is more careful: the excess "could possibly be due to nuclear reaction interference
   effects or might indicate" a new particle. The abstract now says "They proposed that the excess comes from a new boson
   ..., now called X17" (the name X17 came later).
3. **"Similar excesses ... in ⁴He and ¹²C" [2, 3].** Supported: a peak near 115° in ³H(p,e⁺e⁻)⁴He, mass 16.94 MeV [2];
   excesses above 120° in ¹¹B(p,γ)¹²C, mass 17.03 MeV [3].
4. **"a two-arm spectrometer in Hanoi" [4].** Supported: the anomaly was confirmed at Ep = 1225 keV, above 4σ. Note for
   the PAC: A.J. Krasznahorkay and other ATOMKI members are co-authors, so this result is not fully independent of ATOMKI.
   The text does not say it is.
5. **MEG II "measured the same reaction with a tracking detector and found no signal", "R < 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ at 90 % CL" [5].**
   Supported: "No significant signal was found", R₁₈.₁ < 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ (and R₁₇.₆ < 1.8 × 10⁻⁶) for masses 16.5–17.1 MeV,
   with the drift-chamber spectrometer in the COBRA field. 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ / 5.8 × 10⁻⁶ = 2.07, so "2.07 × R_ATOMKI" is right.
6. **"H₂⁺ at 0.883 MV ... as in part of the MEG II beam" [5].** Supported: "When the H₂⁺ ions enter the target, their two
   protons interact with Li nuclei each with roughly half of the H₂⁺ kinetic energy. The H₂⁺ presence in the CW beam
   causes both 440 keV and 1030 keV Be resonances to be populated." (Sec. 2 of the paper.)
7. **PADME "a small excess (about 2σ) near 16.9 MeV in e⁺e⁻ annihilation" [6].** Supported, but the 2σ is the global
   significance ("global p-value of (2.00 ± 0.19)σ"; the local one is larger). The text now says "global significance".
8. **"a simulation by members of MEG II showed that cosmic muons produce a peak near 140° in the ATOMKI spectrometers,
   and that these cosmic-ray coincidences are more frequent than the IPC pairs in the signal window" [7].** The authors
   (Pisa, Pavia, Rome, PSI) are all on the MEG II X17 author list, so "members of MEG II" is right; it is not a
   collaboration paper. The 140° peak appears in models of both the five-arm and the six-arm spectrometer. The cosmic-to-IPC
   ratio above one was shown **for the five-arm set-up under typical ATOMKI running conditions (1 µA, ~300 h)**. The text
   now says so. The authors also state that their results "do not settle the question of the origin of the Atomki
   excesses"; our text does not claim more.
9. **"MX17 ... searches for X17 in ³He(n,e⁺e⁻)⁴He" [8, 9].** Supported: [8] proposes this reaction with a new detection
   set-up; [9] describes the n_TOF measurement with four MPGD TPCs. "It took data in 2026" is our own statement.
10. **"the scintillator-telescope spectrometer that took data on AN2000 in 2023–24" [10].** Supported: ΔE–E telescopes of
    EJ-200 plastic bars read by SiPMs, arranged in clovers; "During 2023 and 2024, an experimental campaign ... took place
    at the AN2000 accelerator (LNL-INFN)".
11. **Section II (2): "ATOMKI saw no anomaly here" (the 17.64 MeV transition), previously without a citation.** Mostly
    supported. [1] reports "a slight deviation ... at large angles above 110°", which it explains by an E1 admixture from
    direct capture. The text now says this and cites [1]. MEG II also set a limit there (R₁₇.₆ < 1.8 × 10⁻⁶).
12. **Section II (3): "ATOMKI also measured at this energy" (Ep = 1.10 MeV) [1].** Supported and understated: the 6.8σ
    deviation of the 2016 paper is the Ep = 1.10 MeV data set (their Fig. 4; they also show 0.80, 1.04 and 1.20 MeV).
    The text now reads "ATOMKI measured its 2016 anomaly, at 6.8 standard deviations, at this beam energy".
